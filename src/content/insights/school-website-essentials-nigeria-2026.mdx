A parent in Kano hears about your school from a colleague. Before she calls, before she visits, she does one thing: she searches for you on her phone. What she finds in the next thirty seconds decides whether you ever hear from her.

That's why a school website in 2026 isn't a digital brochure. It's your front gate, your admissions office and your notice board, open all night. Here are the seven things it needs.

## 1. It loads fast on a cheap phone

Most Nigerian parents browse on budget Android phones over mobile data. If your homepage takes ten seconds to appear, many of them are gone before it does.

A good school website should load in about two seconds on a 3G connection. That means compressed, properly sized images (not a 6MB photo of the assembly hall), minimal scripts, and a design built for small screens first. Ask any web designer you're considering to show you their sites on a slow connection, not just on office Wi-Fi.

## 2. Admissions that happen online

If parents still have to buy a form at the gate, you're losing every family who can't easily get to you: parents in other cities, diaspora families, busy professionals.

Online admissions let parents apply from their phones in minutes, upload documents, pay the form fee, and book an entrance assessment. Your team gets every application in one dashboard instead of a pile of paper.

## 3. A result checker that just works

Result day is the busiest day on your website. A proper result checker accepts scratch-card PINs or portal logins, limits how many times a PIN can be used, and shows results clearly on a phone, with a downloadable report card that prints well.

Grading should follow the formats your parents and teachers already understand, such as WAEC/NECO-style grades and class positions, rather than something invented by the software.

## 4. Fee payments that reconcile themselves

Screenshots of bank alerts in WhatsApp are not a payment system. Integrating Paystack or Flutterwave lets parents pay by card, transfer or USSD, and each payment is matched to the right student automatically. Receipts go out instantly, and your bursar can see who has paid without chasing anyone.

## 5. News and notices that staff can update

A website that was last updated two terms ago tells parents the school isn't paying attention. Your staff should be able to post news, PTA notices, events and photos from a simple dashboard in a few minutes, without calling a developer.

Make every post easy to share on WhatsApp with a proper preview image, because that's where your parents will see it.

## 6. Honest, specific content

Parents want to know what makes your school different, what fees look like, what a school day is like and who the leadership team are. Stock photos of children who don't attend your school, and vague claims about "excellence", don't build trust.

Real photos of your classrooms, labs and events, clear information about admissions and fees, and a few words from your principal will do more than any slogan.

## 7. You own it

Your domain (ideally a .edu.ng or .com.ng address), your content and your data should belong to your school, not to whoever built the site. Make sure the domain is registered in the school's name, that you can get a copy of your content, and that you aren't locked into one provider forever.

## Where to start

You don't need everything at once. Many schools start with a fast, beautiful website and online admissions, then add a result checker, fee payments and a parent portal as they grow.
