Ask most people what an "AI tutor" looks like and they'll describe a chat window connected to a powerful server somewhere, answering any question in seconds. That works well in an office with fibre broadband. It works much less well for a student in a town where the network drops every afternoon and data is expensive.

At Edgetechnical we build AI for education, including our own exam-prep app, StudyWise AI. Here's what we've learned about what good AI exam prep looks like for Nigerian students.

## AI doesn't have to live in the cloud

Until recently, useful AI models were too big to run anywhere but large data centres. That's changing quickly. Smaller, efficient models can now run directly on a smartphone, including the affordable Android phones that most students actually own.

On-device AI changes the picture for students with poor connectivity:

- **It works offline.** Once the app and its model are downloaded, a student can study and talk to the tutor with no network at all.
- **It's cheaper to use.** Students aren't burning data on every question.
- **It's more private.** More of the student's work stays on their own phone.

The trade-off is that on-device models are smaller than the largest cloud models, so the product has to be designed carefully around what they do well: explaining, questioning and practising within a defined syllabus.

## The best tutors don't give answers

The biggest risk with AI in education isn't that it's wrong. It's that it's too helpful. A tool that hands students the answer to every question helps them finish homework, but not pass exams.

Good human tutors work differently. They ask questions. They give hints. They let the student struggle a little, because that's where learning happens. This approach is often called Socratic tutoring, and AI can do it well when it's designed to.

In StudyWise, if a student asks why a stone falls faster than a feather, the tutor doesn't explain air resistance straight away. It asks what would happen if both were dropped in a tube with no air. The student works it out, and remembers it.

## Different students need different tutors

Some students want patient, step-by-step explanations. Others want rapid-fire practice questions before a test. Some benefit from a strict examiner who marks like the real thing.

Multi-persona tutoring lets students switch between these styles, so the same app supports them whether they're learning a topic for the first time or revising the night before.

## Practice should look like the real exam

Exam preparation is most effective when practice resembles the actual exam: the same types of questions, the same time pressure, the same marking. AI can generate explanations for every practice question, so a wrong answer becomes a lesson rather than just a red mark.

## Teachers and parents stay in the picture

AI exam prep works best alongside teachers, not instead of them. Schools can use it to give exam classes extra practice, and teachers can see which topics a class is struggling with.

## Responsible by design

Any AI product used by young people should take privacy seriously. In Nigeria, that means complying with the Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023: collecting only what's needed, protecting it properly, and being clear about how it's used.

## What this means for your school

AI exam prep is no longer limited to students with fast internet and expensive devices. With offline, on-device AI and tutoring designed to build understanding, it can reach students in every part of the country.
